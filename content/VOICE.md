# Terravion — house voice

Read this before writing or editing a single line. It overrides any habit of
"SEO writing". `content/BRIEF.md` still governs facts; this governs voice.

## Who is speaking

A developer who has assembled land in West Hyderabad for years and has watched
buyers get hurt by paperwork. The writing carries the authority of four people:
a senior architect, an urban planner, a property lawyer, and an infrastructure
analyst. It never sounds like a marketing department.

## The rules

**Short sentences.** Most under twenty words. Vary length, but the default is
short. Confidence does not need subordinate clauses.

**Lead with the specific.** Never open with a windup. Not "Hyderabad's real
estate market has grown rapidly in recent years." Open on the concrete claim:
"Shankarpally has a railway station. Very few plotted corridors around
Hyderabad do."

**Every paragraph earns its place.** If a paragraph could be deleted without
losing information, delete it. Three tight paragraphs beat eight padded ones.

**No stacked adjectives.** "Premium luxury world-class gated community" is four
words doing one word's work.

**Concede things.** Real expertise names the downside. If a corridor has a
water-table question, say so. Nothing builds trust faster than a developer who
tells you what to check.

**Explain mechanism, not adjective.** Don't say a location is "well
connected" — say what road it touches and where that road goes. Don't say a
layout is "premium" — say the roads are 40 feet and the drainage is
underground.

**Numbers only where verified.** Facts from BRIEF.md, or publicly established
infrastructure. Never invent a price trend, an appreciation percentage, an
absorption rate, or a "studies show". Where a number would need verification,
write around it or tell the reader where to look it up.

## Banned outright

These phrases and their relatives do not appear anywhere:

- "premium plotted communities", "future ready", "best investment"
- "your dream home / dream plot", "nestled", "boasts", "unlock", "elevate"
- "state-of-the-art", "world-class", "unparalleled", "bespoke", "curated"
- "In today's fast-paced world", "It is no secret that", "Look no further"
- "game changer", "hidden gem", "must-have", "one-stop", "seamless journey"
- Rhetorical questions as openers ("Looking for a plot in Shankarpally?")
- Any sentence that begins "Whether you are a…"
- Closing paragraphs that summarise what the article just said

## Structure

- No introduction that announces the article. Begin inside the subject.
- Headings are statements, not labels. "The water question" beats "Water
  Supply". "What the ring road actually did" beats "ORR Benefits".
- No two pieces open the same way. Vary the entry: a scene, a document, a
  number, a common mistake, a piece of history.
- End on a judgement or an instruction, not a recap.

## A calibration example

Weak, generic:

> Shankarpally is emerging as one of the most sought-after destinations for
> premium plotted developments in West Hyderabad. With excellent connectivity
> and world-class infrastructure, it offers the perfect opportunity for
> investors looking to unlock exceptional value.

House voice:

> Shankarpally sits twenty minutes past the point where Hyderabad stops
> feeling like a city. It has a railway station, which matters more than most
> buyers realise — rail services survive downturns that road projects do not.
> The land here is still farmed. That is the whole argument: you are buying
> before the corridor arrives, not after.
